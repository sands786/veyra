# Veyra Judge Cut — Rejected Master Diagnosis

## Verdict

The 112-second master is technically compliant but editorially weak. It reads as a branded product montage, not as a decisive hackathon demonstration. The core failure is not the visual polish of the app: it is that the film spends too much time **asserting** privacy and coordination while too little time **proving** a consequential user outcome on screen.

## Concrete failures

| Area | What failed | Why it weakened the film | Rebuild rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | The opening logo holds for nearly ten seconds. | Judges see brand before product value, consuming the first ten percent of the runtime. | Limit the open to 2–3 seconds and cut directly into the trigger event. |
| Payroll | The visible actions mostly alter small labels and status text. | The interaction has no visual consequence proportional to the voiceover’s promise. | Use one clearly observable before/after outcome and visually isolate the changed state. |
| Primitives | A text-heavy “three primitives” view reads like a slide. | It tells rather than demonstrates. | Show a live disclosure choice: what becomes shareable and what remains hidden. |
| Markets | The markets view is introduced as another surface rather than as the consequence of an operator decision. | The viewer cannot infer why the workflow matters. | Tie a private bid, policy, or public aggregate result to the action that created it. |
| Launchpad | A simulated release produces only a low-energy progress/status update. | The film lacks a climax and exposes no meaningful coordination outcome. | Use the strongest visible state transition in the Launchpad simulation and describe it explicitly as simulated. |
| Narrative | Product modules appear as a feature tour with no connective decision. | The video feels like a generic montage. | Use a single story: prepare a private coordination record, choose disclosure, set a private market policy, then govern a release. |
| Repetition | Payroll footage returns late in the edit without a substantially different result. | Repetition makes the product breadth feel thin. | Never revisit a surface unless the state is visibly and meaningfully different. |
| Closing | The twelve-second static logo is well-composed but still too long. | Branding becomes dead air immediately after the climax. | Keep the final logo to 2–3 seconds after the last visible result. |

## Required rebuild language

> The replacement must be **screen-led, outcome-led, and feature-scarce**. It should show four actions that visibly change state, not four sections of the product. Teaser branding may frame the film, but it cannot substitute for proof.

The revised edit will use only controls that genuinely exist in the running Veyra interface. Local Demo Mode will remain visibly labeled and narrated as simulated. Any Mainnet reference will stop at the actual boundary: wallet approval, returned transaction hash, and confirmed receipt—not recipient private-note delivery.

## Target structure

| Time | Job | Evidence on screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Immediate premise | Short teaser lockup, then direct cut to a live workspace state. |
| 3–22s | Payroll consequence | One route action with a clear local state/receipt transition and visible simulation marker. |
| 22–39s | Privacy proof | Private Primitives disclosure selection that visibly differentiates revealed versus withheld information. |
| 39–57s | Market policy | A private-market policy/bid interaction followed by a visible aggregate-only or gate outcome. |
| 57–78s | Launchpad climax | A clearly simulated milestone/governance transition with visible changed readiness or release state. |
| 78–94s | Trust boundary | A concise technical proof screen that states exactly what a wallet and confirmed receipt establish. |
| 94–100s | Brand resolve | A 2–3 second clean Veyra closing lockup; no caption or UI collision. |
