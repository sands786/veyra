# Veyra Hackathon Demo Rebuild Blueprint

## The standard

The new cut must feel like a founder-led, high-signal product demonstration rather than a narrated slideshow. A judge should understand the problem in the first ten seconds, see **real Veyra surfaces** operate in the next two minutes, and leave with a clear answer to three questions: what Veyra coordinates, why the privacy boundary is credible, and what is genuinely verified on Mainnet.

The captioned edition will be accessible without becoming visually noisy. Captions are a **small lower-third accessibility layer**, not a title card, a paragraph, or a substitute for product imagery. The clean edition remains the primary submission master.

| Element | Rejected cut | Rebuild standard |
|---|---|---|
| Primary visual | Long static screenshots | Real interactive screen sequences, deliberate scrolls, and controlled camera moves |
| Captions | Full-frame, paragraph-length karaoke text | Two-line maximum lower-third captions, 38–44 px, 75–82% width, timed to a single spoken thought |
| Cursor | Absent | Enlarged smooth cursor, 160–190 ms easing, a soft vermilion click ripple, and visible intent before every interaction |
| Motion | Continuous generic zoom | 130–150% focus zoom only when attention moves to a real UI control, then hold 0.8–1.4 seconds and ease out |
| Chapters | Static labels over a screenshot | Five-second motion-led chapter bridges with one idea, then immediate product proof |
| Audio | Narration dominates a still image | Clear narration over a ducked score, with quiet click, panel, and confirmation sound design |

## The product-film structure

The master stays under three minutes and follows the requested hackathon order. The shot durations below are maximums, not padding. Any dead load, form filling, or repeated navigation is removed through aggressive jump cuts.

## Cursor and interaction choreography

Every visible cursor move must answer a judge’s question. The pointer appears 0.35–0.55 seconds before a click, decelerates into the target, holds for 0.18 seconds, then triggers a 0.45-second vermilion radial ripple. It never teleports, shakes, wanders, or clicks empty space.

Scrolls are used only when they reveal a material second state of the same workflow. Each scroll begins with a brief 100–120 px anticipation movement, runs at a consistent eased velocity, and ends with 0.8 seconds of stillness on the revealed evidence. A clip may contain at most one primary focus zoom at a time. Chapter changes use a 120–180 ms directional whip or a short crossfade, never a 3D transition, page curl, cube, or unmotivated light flash.

## Frame design and background

The product recording sits within a 16:9 graphite window on a soft deep-tide-to-obsidian gradient. The application window has a 20–24 px radius, a restrained soft shadow, and a thin vermilion/ivory edge treatment. It never floats on a bare desktop. The background drifts by only 8–14 px across a long shot so the product remains the focal plane.

Chapter labels are not caption blocks. They enter for 1.0–1.6 seconds at the upper left as a mono eyebrow and a large two-to-four word sentence, then dissolve before the screen interaction begins. Product callouts use one label and one arrow at a time. All typography follows Veyra’s graphite, moon-ivory, and restrained vermilion system.

## Caption accessibility system

The clean export contains no subtitles. The captioned export uses a compact lower-third glass panel positioned 68 px above the lower safe area. It may display a maximum of two lines and roughly nine to fourteen words at once. The current spoken word receives a vermilion emphasis, but the panel advances by short phrase—not full-sentence—groups. Captions fade in over 120 ms and fade out over 100 ms; they never cover key controls, the sidebar, a confirmation, or a data table.

## Audio and pacing

The voiceover is the authority. The score sits approximately 18–22 dB below narration and ducks a further 5–7 dB on dense technical explanations. A muted click sound accompanies a primary click; a soft low confirmation tone accompanies a receipt/state reveal; a light riser bridges only chapter transitions. There are no loud swells, cash-register effects, generic whooshes on every edit, or music-only stretches longer than two seconds.

The target rhythm is a meaningful change of view every 2.5–4.5 seconds. A shot may last up to six seconds only when the judge is reading a real proof or watching the outcome of a meaningful interaction. Every scene either demonstrates a real product capability or explains a constraint that protects the user; nothing is filler.

## Non-negotiable truth boundary

The rebuilt video will show the three verified STRK20 privacy-pool receipts only as **public Mainnet receipt evidence**. It will not assert that a public hash proves private recipient-note delivery. It will say that the wallet owns signing, Veyra records the returned hash once, and verification requires a Starknet receipt. It will not show a public-transfer fallback, a generic invoke, fabricated settlement, fake balances, invented counterparties, or seeded customer testimonials.
